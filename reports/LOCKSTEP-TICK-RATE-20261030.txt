Lockstep tick rate and frame cost, 2026-10-30
=============================================

All of this is offline work on the gate sources and on logs of earlier runs. Nothing
here was run live. Numbers marked "measured" come from FRAME_TIMING rows of census runs on
the Windows PC; "estimate" means reasoned from them, to be checked by the runs in section 7.

1. Short version
----------------
- The world runs slow because dt is pinned at 1/60 s while a tick costs 31-37 ms. Fixed
  1/30 s puts the play stack at about real time today with no wire change. Recommended
  first: native_gate_tick_hz = 30.
- The relay's frame numbers were wrong in a way that hid this: time.monotonic on Windows
  (Python 3.12) is GetTickCount64 with 15.6 ms steps, so every period came out as about
  15, 31, 47 or 62 ms and the median was 31 for almost every configuration, Pbare included,
  whose own frame is 23.6 ms. Use FRAME_TIMING or seconds/ticks for anything older. The relay
  now uses time.perf_counter, and census_perf adds the mean.
- Pathing already computes in parallel. What is serial is one thing: the MoveWorker waits
  for the pass right after kicking it. That wait exists so the pass does not race the
  universe update, and it is what costs. Rather than move the wait, the cut is to do less in
  the pass: stock gives a batch about 1 ms of full updates and the rest cheap updates in
  round robin; the gate lifted that budget because it was clock-based, so every agent gets a
  full update every tick. native_gate_path_budget = N brings stock's round robin back with a
  count instead of the clock, on both gates. On the VPS this is the largest item in the frame.
- Mass traffic cannot overlap FrameTick (it reads the published view bank and syncs game
  time at its start). The region pass joins at the same point and overlaps it, so the join
  costs the longer of the two: remove traffic alone and the region join goes from 0.5 to 2.7
  ms, with no gain (ac-Base, ac-MT). They do not have to run every tick:
  native_gate_pass_stride runs both every Nth, in phase.
- Reads: native_gate_fast_reads replaces 10-15 thousand ReadProcessMemory calls per tick with a
  copy under SEH, the Windows answer to the Linux gate's 218a90e1.
- Dynamic dt is feasible and sound but gives up reproducibility from inputs alone. Later, if
  weak hosts need it.

2. The tick rate (done)
-----------------------
native_gate_tick_hz: 60 (default), 40, 30, 20. The constant lives in lockstep_tick.h, shared by
both gates. native_gate_tick_pace (default true with an explicit rate) holds FrameTick to the
client's own schedule so a cheap tick does not run faster than real time; a late client
catches up to 250 ms, further behind it re-bases. Pacing runs after the permit and after the
report left, so no other client waits for the sleep, and it changes no simulated value. The
relay takes --tick-hz and stops on a client at another rate at the first step.

Changed to follow the rate
  lockstep_gate.c: patched dt word, gate_sites_owned, clock check, start
  linux/gate_linux.c: the dt load now reads the gate's own read-only page at rates other than 60
  lockstep_attention.h, lockstep_gravidar.h, lockstep_nav.h, lockstep_nav_cadence.h,
  lockstep_shape.h: "one frame ago" deadlines
  lockstep_pilot_device.h: throttle ramp; lockstep_pilot_predict.h: prediction step
  native_gate_relay.py: timestep check, summary; native_gate_probe.lua: arms before the start
  check_gate_options.py, validate_native_gate.py, census_perf.py, native_gate_ssh.py,
  run_native_gate.py, check_lockstep_gate_linux.py (two scenarios)

Refusing any rate but 60 (the contract is written for 1/60)
  lockstep_portable_windows.h, lockstep_player_getup_native.h, and their tools:
  native_getup_diagnostic.py:99, native_portable_coordinator.py:198,
  validate_native_portable.py:79,81, test_native_gate_ssh_network.py:19-20

Separate scheduler: Rust x4mp-agent has fixed_dt(hz) for 60/30/20 (pinned.rs, sched_live.rs,
scheduler.rs); not part of the gate. check_lockstep_gate_linux_sites.py pins the 1/60 constant
of the game itself; that stays.

Counted in ticks, unchanged, so their time doubles at 30 Hz:
  native_gate_pilot_delay (default 3 ticks, must be at least lookahead+2): 50 ms becomes 100 ms
  pilot window ticks, PILOT_CAMERA_RETRY 60, PGETUP_TICK 30
  HITN_EVICT_TICKS 1800 ("30 s" becomes 60 s), NOCT_DELAY_DEFAULT 30
  FPENV_SAMPLE_TICKS 600; probe samples tick%60 (HighwaySample, Boost/Travel Sample, TourStep),
  tick%600 status rows
  world_hash_stride, status_stride, trace_stride in configs: same ticks, twice the game time
None of these changes behaviour on a client's own; reduce the stride configs if you want the
same game-time density. Time-based constants (PNAT timers now+0.5 and so on) follow by themselves.
The stock record's timing is capture16 ticks + pilot delay: it stays tick-based.
Mentions of 60 Hz in older reports are history and were left.

3. A dt carried by the permit (evaluated, not built)
----------------------------------------------------
Feasible. A permit for tick P is issued when tick P-1-k is complete everywhere, so NGGO can
carry dt(P). The relay measures the slowest client's frame, sets dt, clamps to 1/60..1/20. The
hook writes the word (Linux: the gate's page, made writable) before FrameTick, and the relay
checks delta==dt(tick). The control lag is k+1 ticks; quantize and limit the step per tick.

What sees a varying dt: X4 is variable-dt by origin (dt clamp 1.0), so flight, AI, agents and
physics take dt as a parameter, and Jolt's warm-start ratio handles a change. The census's
fixed-dt-related bugs were about stale "time-1/60" comparisons in gate modules: all of those
now call gate_tick_dt(), which would become "this tick's dt". actor_all's seeds include dt bits,
fine as long as they agree. Tick-keyed cadences stay ticks.

Sound for lockstep: every client applies the same bits at the same tick; dt is one more agreed
input. The costs:
- The world is no longer reproducible from inputs and a snapshot unless the dt log is kept in
  the journal (it would have to be). Census runs stop being comparable: two runs at the same
  seed walk different dt series, unless dt is replayed.
- The dt depends on the machine's load, so a hitch on one client bends everyone's physics step.
- Feedback: a larger dt makes more per-tick work (more agents wake, more contact pairs), gain
  below 1 but not 0; needs damping.
- Rendering stays one frame per tick and becomes uneven; a fixed rate with pacing is even.
- It needs a protocol field and a Linux and Windows change of the dt write path.

Recommendation: fixed 1/30 now. No wire change, frame pacing is locked, runs stay comparable.
Use the cuts below to go to 1/40 where the machine has room. Build dynamic later only if weak
hosts or the VPS need it; with k+1 lag it answers load swings slowly anyway.

Feel: display rate equals tick rate today. 30 Hz means 30 FPS and 100 ms pilot delay at
lookahead 1 (3 ticks); lookahead 0 gives 2 ticks = 67 ms. At 40 Hz: 40 FPS, 75 ms. A rate of 60 with
slow motion is smoother per frame but it is a 45% world, which is not playable either way. Display
interpolation between ticks is the separate way out, and it is not part of this work.

The same configuration gave 29.65 and 27.31: the run-to-run noise is about 2 ms. Measured
against that, removing created identities, the RNG group, the FP group or the diagnostics
shows nothing beyond noise (diagnostics: about 0.35 ms, visible in the probe column). So those
are not where the time is. The pathing group has not been removed yet (section 6): its cost below
comes from status rows, not from removal.

ac-MT is the instructive one. Without mass traffic the join is unchanged at 2.65 ms: the
region join, 0.5 ms in the base, becomes 2.7 ms. The passes overlap, and only the longer one
costs anything. In short runs region is the longer; by tick 2000 of a long run traffic is, which is
why region showed 38 us at tick 18000. That figure is what is left after traffic, not its length.
ac-Reg is the first removal above the noise: 2.65 ms, of which 0.6 is the join (traffic alone
takes 2.0) and 1.8 is in "rest". One run, and ten keys went together (region pass, region
fields and RNG, the camera keys, the viewpoint policy), so the 1.8 may belong to any of them.
ac-Grav: 1.3 ms, inside the noise, but it carries 4,500 of the 10,800 reads per frame, so the
gravidar group is the biggest user of native_gate_fast_reads. ac-Cam and ac-Ord: nothing, or
slower, which is the noise again.

Over one long run (play22-long, 3000-tick bins): outside 29.6 to 33.2, pre 3.2 to 4.2, created
0.19 to 0.5, join 2.5 to 3.2, reads 10.7k to 15.4k. World growth drives the rise. Mean period
36.9 ms against a median near 33: hitches are about 10% of the time.

  pathing pass        3.2-3.5 ms wall, 30 jobs, about 9,740 agents

  script RNG self     40-50 us

VPS Linux, 2 instances on 8 cores at nice 10, no GPU (relay period, Linux clock, so not
quantized): vps-linux-4 median 23.4 ms, vps-linux-5 with serial pathing and path_determinism=3
median 41.2 ms. The status rows of vps-linux-5 give path determinism 2.5 ms (collect 1.0, refresh
1.4), so about 15 ms of the 18 is the wait for the pass, with 10,028 agents on full updates.
All of this predates the Linux fast read, so the share of the pass needs measuring again.

Ranking by what can be removed:
  1. Pathing pass: 3.3 ms here, about 15 ms on the VPS.
  2. Traffic and region join: 2.7-3.3 ms, only together.
  3. Reads: up to 3.8 ms CPU, partly wall. Linux has its fast read since 218a90e1 (a frame there
     went from 88 to 39 ms); Windows gets it with native_gate_fast_reads.
  4. Path determinism: 1 ms here, 2.5 ms on the VPS. Stays; it is what makes stamps agree.
  5. Probe sampling: about 0.4 ms by ablation, diagnostics only.
Regression across older runs does not separate the keys beyond about 1 ms: they are confounded.
None of this explains the whole 21 ms of "rest" that Pbare has too. That is the engine and the
renderer, and sim_us (new in FRAME_TIMING) will say which of the two sets the period.

5. Can it stay parallel and still be deterministic?
---------------------------------------------------
Pathing. The batch jobs are already parallel with travel_snapshot: each job builds a private
command list, the order module applies the lists in canonical order after the join, and
path_determinism stamps in ID order afterwards. So "commit in canonical order, compute in
parallel" is what runs today. "Serial pathing" only means the MoveWorker does not go on with the
frame while the pass runs. Three options:
  a. Fewer full updates: native_gate_path_budget (built). Stock's own mechanism, by count. The
     decision depends on the batch list and cursor; walk= in PATH_DETERMINISM shows whether
     those agree across clients (census_perf --agree PATH_DETERMINISM:walk). About a third of
     the agents stay on full updates at N=100 with 30 batches of about 330. Estimate: pass 3.3 to
     about 1.5 ms here, and about 15 to 5 ms on the VPS.
  b. Let the pass overlap the universe update as stock does. The pass reads world state while the
     frame writes it, and results depend on who was first. Needs a full audit of the read set.
     Rejected for now.
  c. Run the pass in the MoveWorker's idle gap: kick it at the end of frame N, join at the start of
     frame N+1. The pass reads game time at its start, so it would need the N+1 time patched in, the
     pre-release hooks moved, and a join before FrameTick so it never overlaps the publish. Estimate
     2.5-3 ms saved here. Invasive; the next step if (a) is not enough.

Mass traffic and region. Traffic's thread reads the published view bank and syncs game time at
its start, so its pass must end before FrameTick's publish unless it is made independent of both.
Region joins at the same point and overlaps it. Their outputs are already deterministic
through their per-pass seeds and the join. So they stay parallel with each other and with the
main thread today; what costs is that FrameTick waits for the longer one. They can only be run
less often: native_gate_pass_stride (built) holds both, in phase, and saves about (1-1/N) of
2.7-3.3 ms. A stride on traffic alone would save nothing, as ac-MT shows.

6. The ablation queue
---------------------
Round 1 (ab-*) stopped at arming for Path, MT, Reg, Grav, Ord and Cam, because keys that stayed
need the keys removed. Round 2 (ac-*) fixed those except pathing, which is the one group still
without a number on Windows. To remove it, take these out as well:
  native_gate_radar_serial, native_gate_spline_join, native_gate_tempzone_order
tools/dev/check_gate_options.py on the installed config names them before a launch.
With the same-config noise at 2 ms, run a base between them or each one twice.

7. What to run
--------------
All on the current play stack, no captures, 1203 ticks unless noted. Run each twice: the noise
is about 2 ms. Read with census_perf --native; since this commit its frames row is no longer
quantized and has a mean. simulated_per_wall is the number that counts.

a. Tick rate. native_gate_tick_hz = 30, relay --tick-hz 30 (run_native_gate.py and native_gate_ssh.py
   take --tick-hz; a private launcher script has to pass it on). Expect simulated_per_wall near
   1.0, frame period 33.3 ms, pace_us above 0 on cheap ticks, verdict pass. Also one long
   soak for the world hash, as dt is a new input to everything. Options that refuse rates other
   than 60: portable Windows and player getup. native_gate_pilot_delay stays at 3 ticks, which
   is now 100 ms.
b. native_gate_path_budget = 100, then 50 and 200. Add --agree PATH_DETERMINISM:walk to
   census_perf: it must report differing=0. Compare TRAVEL_SNAPSHOT pass_us, sim_us and the world
   hash. If walk differs between clients, the batch lists do not agree and the budget cannot be
   used until they do: that is the one way this can fail. Also on the VPS with the vps-linux-5 keys.
   Estimate there: 41 to about 30 ms.
   This changes the world against earlier baselines: agents get a full update every 3 ticks or so
   at N=100, about what stock gives them, not every tick. Look at a play run for behaviour too.
c. native_gate_pass_stride = 2, then 3 (with mass traffic and region pass on 'sync'). join_ms should
   fall to about 1/N; MASS_TRAFFIC stride_skips rises. World hash must hold. Asteroids and traffic
   around the camera now update less often: look at a play run.
d. native_gate_fast_reads = true. reads in FRAME_TIMING then counts only reads above 4 KiB and should
   fall to near 0. Look at rest_ms and at TRAVEL_SNAPSHOT pass_us.
e. Read sim_us next to outside_us in any of these. If sim_us is close to outside_us, the
   simulation sets the period and further cuts here pay. If it is well below, the main thread and
   the renderer set it and the cuts have reached their limit.
f. The pathing ablation of section 6 with the extra keys removed.
g. If b to d hold: all at 30 Hz as a play run, then try native_gate_tick_hz = 40 (25 ms budget).

What is not done: the pass stride on the Linux gate (no mass traffic module there yet). Option c of
section 5 (pass in the idle gap). A dt carried by the permit.
