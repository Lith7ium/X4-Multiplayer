LOCKSTEP LOCAL PREDICTION AND HIT CLAIMS
29 October 2026 | static analysis + offline implementation; no X4 launched, no live result yet

SUMMARY

Goal (user): the local player's own ship responds instantly on their screen,
like a client-side-predicted shooter, while the deterministic lockstep
simulation applies their pilot record d ticks later on every client (pilot by
wire, reports/NATIVE-LOCKSTEP-PILOTING-20261029.txt). Agreed model (user,
via the coordinator): the owning client runs "a little ahead"; it decides hits
that involve its own predicted ship, incoming and outgoing, and those
decisions enter the shared simulation deterministically as inputs; the AI and
every other simulation system keep reacting to the authoritative, delayed
lockstep state.

Delivered on contrib/ferrite-lockstep-prediction, rebased onto the coordinator
head ec0c35e5 (default off, nothing changes without the new options):
- lockstep_pilot_predict.h: the prediction model (pure, unit tested), an
  OBSERVE mode that predicts the local slot ship d ticks ahead every tick and
  measures the error against the simulated pose when it arrives (plus two
  baselines), read-only checks of the render precondition, and a PRESENT mode
  that draws the local slot ship and everything relative to its cockpit at the
  predicted pose, scoped to VBI slot 10 inside the existing render RNG split
  wrapper, strict restore, stop reason 101. No thunk (recipe count stays 55),
  no new code patch, one call pair added to the render RNG split wrapper.
- lockstep_hit_claims.h: hit-claim prototype (codec, input blob multiplexing
  for pilot/hits/orders, staging, deterministic integer validation, conflict
  policies, receipt). Pure; compiled into the test build only; no engine hook.
- Probe wiring (native_gate_pilot_predict*), sandbox builder options
  (--predict1/--predict2/--predict-raw), check_gate_options rule, tests.
Not implemented: the camera-table rewrite (stage 2 of present), presentation
of the player's own projectiles, and every native hook of the hit claims.
They are designed below with the sites the static passes found.

1. WHAT "ZERO LATENCY" MEANS UNDER LOCKSTEP

Let S(t) be the simulated pose of the local slot ship at tick t. The record
captured at tick t applies at t+d on every client, so the simulated ship is
the latency-free native trajectory shifted by d: if N(t) is the pose a native
(unshared) game would show at t, then S(t) = N(t-d), i.e. N(T) = S(T+d).
Showing the predicted S(T+d) in frame T is therefore exactly the native,
latency-free ship motion, only that the rest of the world (simulated at T) is
d ticks behind it. That is the "client instance a little ahead": the player
sees their own ship and camera at T+d against a world at T. With d = 3
(50 ms) the lead is v*50 ms: 15 m at 300 m/s, 0 when stationary. The part
that depends on the pending inputs is small (about 0.2 m and 0.4 degrees over
50 ms for a fighter at full acceleration); most of the presented offset is
the velocity lead, which the model gets right by construction.

2. PREDICTION MODEL (implemented, lockstep_pilot_predict.h)

State per tick T (UI thread = MoveWorker, slot 6, after this tick's capture):
- S(T): the ship's pathing read bank (controller+0x30 slots, index 0x38f4ee4,
  0x1a0 stride; +0x00 position, +0x10/+0x20/+0x30 local x/y/z axes in parent
  (zone) coordinates, as the portable state encodes it), guarded reads only.
- v(T): body rates from the finite difference S(T-1) -> S(T) in S(T-1)'s
  local frame: linear m/s along local x/y/z, angular rad/s about local x/y/z
  (log map of R(T) R(T-1)^T). The bank's own velocity fields (+0x40..+0x5f)
  are logged raw but not used: their frame is not established.
- The h records that apply at T+1..T+h: wire source = this client's pending
  ring through gate.pilot_pending's text (h = wire delay; a missing record
  holds the previous one, counted); script source = pilot_script (h set by
  native_gate_pilot_predict_horizon, every slot predicted: a no-human test).
- Targets: each record maps onto the ship's own Agent limits exactly as the
  substitution does (pilot_target: linear x/y/z, angular pitch/yaw/roll).
Model, per axis i (6 axes), one step per tick:
    dv = clamp(gain_i * (sign_i * target_i - v_i), -down_i, +up_i)
then p += (v_lin . R) dt, R = Exp(v_ang dt) R. P(T) = S(T) advanced h steps
estimates S(T+h). Each tick restarts from the latest simulated state, so a
model error never accumulates beyond h steps.
Online fit (driven ticks only: the frame table says driving and both applied
counters grew by exactly 3 this tick): the sign from the correlation of
target and velocity change (an axis whose sign flips is refitted); a sample
whose change reaches 90 % of the current limit estimate updates up/down (rise
0.5 towards larger, settle 0.05 towards smaller changes), any other sample
updates gain (exponential mean, alpha 0.05, unbiased under the float32
position noise of zone coordinates); samples with an error below 4x noise
(0.5 m/s linear, 1e-3 rad/s angular), moving away from the target or
overshooting it by more than 25 % (impulses, collisions, another flight model)
are not fitted. An axis with fewer than 20 samples keeps constant velocity.
Display smoothing: displayed = P(T) plus a correction. The correction takes
the jump between the continuation of the previous display path (its end
state advanced one step with the newest target) and the new P(T), and decays
with tau (native_gate_pilot_predict_tau, default 100 ms); above 50 m or
0.5 rad it snaps (counted). A new input does not create a jump (the
continuation uses the same newest record), so input response is not smoothed
away. With an unfitted axis the correction partly cancels acceleration lead
(seen in the unit test); keep tau around 50-100 ms.
Resets: an unreadable sample or a zone change (object+0x70) restarts the
history; predictions across a zone change are never compared.
Measured per slot when the target tick arrives: position and angle error of
the model, of constant velocity from S(T) ("dead") and of no prediction
("none" = S(T) itself, i.e. what the delayed picture costs), and of the
smoothed display pose; mean/rms/max and a position histogram (edges 0.01,
0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10 m), the fitted model per axis
(aN=sign/gain/up m/s^2/down m/s^2/samples/refused).

Expected (unit test on a synthetic ship of the model's form, float32 bank,
script maneuvers): model max error < 3 cm and < 1e-3 rad over 3 ticks after
one 600-tick cycle; constant velocity max > 0.2 m in transitions; "none" max
> 10 m at speed. The real solver is not of the model's form (drag, assist,
coupling); the live observe run decides whether the model is good enough.

3. WHERE THE OFFSET IS APPLIED

Render path (RE pass on a private read-only Ghidra copy plus capstone;
static; the layouts the code relies on are re-checked at run time):
- Slot 10 (X4::GameRender::Move 0x121c1f0) renders every camera; Camera::
  vfunc3 0x983160 -> Scene::vfunc915 0x779a00 builds a per-scene context:
  ctx[1] = the camera's zone, ctx[3] = (camera+0x770)->vfunc837, normally the
  player ship's cockpit, ctx[4..0xb] = inverse camera pose relative to ctx[3].
- Every component's model-view is 0x3de630(component, ctx[3]) x ctx[4..0xb].
  0x3de630 is a cached relative transform: it walks parents through view
  cache [A] (A = *0x38f4ee0 on the MoveWorker, verified in the disassembly at
  0x3de677), cached parent at object+0xe0+A*0x80 (vc+0) and a pose block at
  vc+0x28 (PosRot, row 0 = translation; +0x40 scale; +0x50 velocity), and
  composes up to the common ancestor and down into ctx[3]'s frame.
- The ship's own parts (cockpit, turrets, engines, lights) share the ship as
  common ancestor with the cockpit, so they never use the ship's pose block;
  every other object reaches the cockpit through the ship's block.
- The pose blocks are filled in slot 3 (BuildCache 0x3d4a80 -> controller
  GetTransform), and the MoveWorker publish 0xef41e0 swaps 0x38f4ee0 with
  0x2f4b71c afterwards (verified); the main thread later copies them to
  0x38f4eb4/0x2f4b718, the index other threads use. During slot 10 the
  MoveWorker reads [A] = *0x38f4ee0, other threads read the other buffer.
Present mode (implemented, stage 1):
- Slot 6: the observe step also checks, read only, that the ship's block [A]
  equals S(T) (<= 1 cm, rotation <= 1e-4), its cached parent is the ship's
  zone and the blocks of [A] and [1-A] are distinct memory (view_ok,
  view_mismatch, view_parent, view_shared, view_missing, view_deviation).
  These counters are reported in observe mode too: the first observe run
  answers whether present can work at all.
- Slot 10: render_rng_execute (lockstep_render_rng.h) calls
  predict_present_enter() before and predict_present_leave() inside its
  __finally after the original GameRender::Move, MoveWorker only. Enter
  re-checks the block (same pointer rules, still S(T) of this admitted tick),
  saves it and writes the displayed pose (xyz of the four vectors; w kept)
  with a guarded write (WriteProcessMemory on the own process). Leave puts
  S(T) back if the block still holds what was written, also when an exception
  unwinds; a block the engine rewrote meanwhile is left alone (counted
  foreign). A nested enter, an unreadable block or a failed restore is a
  failure: the next gate tick stops with reason 101.
- Effect: all geometry is drawn as seen from the predicted ship and camera in
  cockpit (and, if camera+0x770 is under the ship, external) views; the ship
  itself keeps its cockpit-relative layout. Only this client's slot ship,
  only during slot 10, only while |display - S(T)| <= 200 m and 0.5 rad (travel
  drive is shown simulated, counted present_bounds).
- Requirements: native_gate_render_rng_split = true (the wrapper), pins of
  0x3de630 (0x7e5), 0x979ef0 (0x264), 0xef41e0 (0x117) at arm (verified
  against X4.exe 9.00 in the native-image test together with the three index
  loads), the piloting prototype armed, the client's slot in the manifest.
Not covered by stage 1 (visible artefacts, not desync sources by design):
- Frustum culling (queue+0x300) and LOD (queue+0x290) use the camera table
  (zone space), which stays simulated: objects near screen edges may pop by
  up to the lead; LOD switches shift by the lead distance.
- World-space effects (particles, trails, explosions) and anything drawn from
  the view block's view matrix stay at the simulated camera: up to the lead
  offset against their objects while flying fast.
- TAA/DLSS motion vectors: 0x75169e stores model-views into vc[A]+0x40 as
  history; prediction changes them consistently, but jumps (snaps) ghost.
Stage 2 (designed): rewrite the player camera's table [A] for the zone and
every entry above it during slot 10. Writer 0x979ce0/0x9799f0 (slot 3, index
*0x2f4b71c before the swap, so the same buffer as [A] in slot 10): table at
camera+0x30+A*0x2b0, +0x00 attached component (refcounted), +0x10 entries of
0x50 bytes {component*, pad, PosRot mat4 at +0x10 = camera pose in that
component's frame}, 8 inline (capacity at +0x290, otherwise +0x10 holds a heap
pointer), count +0x2a0; the first entry has component 0 (root). Rewrite
M_X' = M_ship o P o M_zone^-1 o M_X for the zone, its ancestors and the root
entry (o = PosRot composition, applied left first), keep the ship's subtree,
check M_zone ~ M_ship o S first, compare-and-restore after. Open: the view
block cache camera+0x8e0 (filled by Camera::vfunc4 0x983e40 -> 0x983a00,
cleared only by 0x97a160 at the top of each iteration): if slot 6 fills it,
slot 10 reuses blocks built from the simulated table; clearing it ourselves
means a native call that frees allocator chunks. Measure first (observe:
camera+0x8e0+8 at slot-10 entry).

Slot-10 consumers of the ship or camera pose that are not pure rendering
(RE pass): the LOD flag or [comp+0x11c+A*0x80],0x100000 at 0x75049a (drives
BuildCache attachment refresh), the model-view history at vc[A]+0x40, the
render-animator visibility flag, Zone::vfunc693 0x8429a0 welding traffic ->
SpawnEffect 0xd73e00 (IDs, camera distance, RNG), the RNG draw in 0x77b890,
finished camera controllers deleted in Camera::vfunc3, camera+0x890..0x8d0.
All of them already depend on each client's own camera (the existing render
RNG split, bullet and nav cadence and view-point fixes were built for that);
present mode changes their inputs on one client but adds no new kind of
coupling. Whether one of them still reaches the simulation is exactly what the
census run with present on one client measures.

4. IMPACTS, EFFECTS, HUD

Consistent automatically (component geometry through the cockpit chain):
other ships, stations, bullets (U::Bullet components), impact decals and
effects attached to components, shields drawn on the ship.
Not consistent in stage 1 and their mitigations:
- HUD: target brackets, lead indicators, crosshair guidance and distances
  come from simulated state through FFI/UI (slot 6). Brackets of other objects
  follow the drawn world only up to the lead. Mitigation (designed): an FFI-free
  gate.pilot_predict_offset() returning the displayed-minus-simulated
  transform so the HUD Lua can shift its own projections; or stage 2 (most UI
  projection goes through the camera table/view blocks).
- World-space effects and positional audio: simulated camera (stage 2 fixes
  effects; audio keeps the simulated listener, 50 ms is inaudible).
- Own shots: the simulated gun fires at T+d from S(T+d); without more work the
  player sees their shots appear d ticks late and from the simulated muzzle,
  which (with the lead) is where the predicted ship is d ticks later: shots
  look consistent relative to the ship but start late. Designed: present the
  player's own projectiles d ticks ahead (the same scoped pose substitution on
  their pose blocks: their simulated position at T+d), which is also the
  geometry the outgoing hit claims use (section 7); muzzle flashes at fire
  time need presentation-only effects (later).
- Impacts on the own ship: with hit claims, applied at admission (one tick
  after the owner saw them), drawn on the predicted ship: consistent.
- Other players see this client's ship at S(T) (d ticks behind its owner's
  view) and their own ship ahead: normal for predicted netcode.

6. INTERACTION WITH INPUT CAPTURE

The predictor reads only interfaces of the piloting prototype: gate.pilot_
pending's text (capture:apply:hex), the frame table (record, driving,
applied counters, resolved object/agent), pilot_frame_tick, pilot_delay,
pilot_script and the pure helpers pilot_decode/pilot_unhex/pilot_target/
pilot_limits_valid. No capture code was edited; the capture rework can change
its internals freely as long as gate.pilot_pending keeps its text format.
Ordering: the probe calls gate.pilot_predict_frame right after this tick's
capture in slot 6, so the record of tick T is already pending and moves the
predicted ship in frame T (slot 10 of the same frame).
Channels: the record carries processed, ship-relative channels (stick/keys
as rates, mouse steering as cursor offset from the window centre), never a
camera transform. The predicted camera rotates with the predicted ship, so
the player's visual feedback loop (aim with the mouse until the target is
centred) closes on the predicted view, which is the point; the captured
values mean the same with or without prediction. The device sampler reads OS
state only and is unaffected. External/free-look cameras do not steer.

7. HITS: CONSTRAINT, OPTIONS, AGREED DESIGN

(Update: the native side - hooks, keys, suppression, owner evaluation,
injection - and pilot firing are implemented, default off; see
reports/LOCKSTEP-HIT-CLAIMS-NATIVE-20261029.txt.)

Constraint. In lockstep every client simulates the same hits from the same
state. The local ship is presented at S(T+d) but simulated at S(T): a
projectile that visibly hits the presented ship may pass the simulated one and
vice versa. Options:
(a) Presentation-only prediction, hits stay native. Deterministic for free;
    the owner sees shots "go through" or hit "empty space" by up to the lead;
    impact effects appear on the simulated hull d ticks later. Cheap, honest,
    unsatisfying at speed.
(b) Re-time/re-place effects only (draw native impacts on the predicted
    ship, hide misses near it). Cosmetic; the outcome is still native.
(c) AGREED: the owner's ahead instance decides every hit that involves its
    predicted ship and sends the decision as an input; every client applies it
    identically at a fixed apply tick and native detection is suppressed for
    those pairs on every client. What the owner sees is what happens. Cost:
    client authority over those hits (trust), wire bytes, native hooks;
    validation bounds stop impossible claims, they cannot stop a lying client
    within the bounds.

Agreed design (c):
Who decides. Pairs (projectile, target) where the target is a predicted slot
ship (incoming) or the projectile's shooter is a predicted slot ship
(outgoing). The set is the agreed slot manifest, identical everywhere. Every
client's native hit test drops such pairs (a projectile flies on unless a
valid claim consumes it). Everything else stays native.
What the owner evaluates (designed):
- Incoming, frame T: simulated projectiles at T (the world is not shifted)
  against the own ship presented at P(T+d). Equivalent and cheaper: move the
  projectile's segment (last test -> now) by S(T) P(T+d)^-1 into the frame where
  the ship is at S(T) and ray-cast it against that ship's body only, read
  only (Scene_CastRayClosest 0xbc4d0 / System_CastRay 0xbb5d0 on the zone's
  physics system, layer-3 filter as the native test; no physics step runs in
  slot 6). Not yet verified that this query is safe to issue outside
  UpdateFlight.
- Outgoing, frame T: the player's own projectiles presented d ticks ahead
  (their simulated position at T+d) against targets at T.
Claim v1 (implemented codec, 26 bytes): version/kind (incoming, outgoing),
flags 0, low 16 bits of the observe tick, projectile key (portable), target
portable identity (saved or created ID), impact point in the target's local
frame (3 x int16, 1/8 m). Up to 8 per payload, no duplicate projectile.
Input blob multiplexing (implemented): the protocol-3 blob per slot and tick
(<= 240 bytes) keeps the legacy single forms (28-byte pilot record starting
0x01; player orders starting 0x50 'P') and adds a container 'M' (0x4d),
version 1, sections kind/length/payload in ascending kind order: 0x01 pilot,
0x48 'H' hit claims, 0x50 'P' orders. A container holding only a channel that
has a legacy form is refused, so every content has one encoding. The probe's
pilot adapter and the orders adapter still own X4LAN_NATIVE_INPUTS
exclusively; combining them needs one adapter that splits the container
(next step, coordinate with the orders owner).
Timing (implemented). A blob sealed with report r-1 is admitted at r. The
observe tick o (from its low 16 bits) must lie in [r-1-8, r-1]. Incoming
claims apply at r (the earliest tick every client can; the projectile has
moved one tick). Outgoing claims apply at o+d: the projectile, simulated d
ticks behind its presentation, is then where the owner saw it hit; o+d < r
is late. Claims are staged per apply tick (64-tick ring).
Resolution at apply tick t (implemented, every client): claims of t ordered
by (policy rank, observe tick, slot, index), validated against the simulated
state at t, all integer arithmetic (mm, Q30 rotation rows) so the result is
identical across compilers and the Linux build:
  owner      incoming: target = claimant's slot ship; outgoing: projectile
             shooter = claimant's slot ship; never shooter = target
  existence  projectile alive and not consumed; target exists, operational
  point      |local point| <= target radius + 5 m
  distance   |projectile - claimed world point| <= (|v_p|1 + |v_t|1)(d+2)
             + 10 m (L1 speeds bound the Euclidean ones)
  policy     player vs player (shooter and target both slot ships): 'shooter'
             (outgoing decides, incoming refused), 'victim' (the reverse) or
             'first' (both allowed, earlier observe tick first); open decision
             for the owner/lead; default proposal: shooter
The first valid claim applies the native hit and consumes the projectile;
later claims for it are refused as consumed; an apply that fails is refused
and consumes nothing. Every outcome (applied or refused with its reason)
folds into an order-independent receipt (lockstep_seed sum) that belongs in
the declared state, so a validation difference stops the barrier at once.
Stale claims (projectile gone because of range expiry, a native hit on a
non-player object first, or death) are refused on every client; the owner
then shows the hit it predicted and the world disagrees: undo cosmetically
(hit marker cancelled) when the refusal comes back.
Native hooks (RE pass; pins = start + .pdata size, SHA-256 prefixes as
reported, to be re-hashed when implemented):
  observe   ApplyHit 0xd528f0 (0x106b) entry: (bullet, target, hit pose,
            normal, time, first); shooter at bullet+0x2a0. Needs a trampoline
            (thunk recipe count would grow).
  suppress  result filter after the nearest cast at 0xd4fc61 (UpdateFlight
            0xd4f240, 0x12a2) and the all-hits cast at 0xd4f766: blank the
            0x38 result block (fraction -1, component 0) when the pair
            involves a predicted ship. Code redirect. (Alternative: the native
            two-body ignore of XBodyFilter 0xbbad0; UpdateFlight fills one.)
  inject    call ApplyHit 0xd528f0(live bullet, target, hit pose, normal,
            time, first=1) on the MoveWorker at a deterministic point of tick
            t (a walk barrier in component order, as the parallel combat line
            does), reusing native damage (ApplyDamage 0x51f7f0, shield
            0x791760), events (PostAttackedEvents 0x51ff10) and effects.
  identity  a portable projectile key at creation (Zone::vfunc930 0x83b500 /
            BulletSetup 0xd50f30, MoveWorker, walk order): proposed
            lockstep_seed(domain, creation tick, shooter portable ID, weapon
            index, per-(shooter, tick) ordinal) (hit_projectile_key); the
            bullet registry of lockstep_bullet.h does this for fixture guns.
  cadence   native_gate_bullet_cadence='every' required (the native test
            interval depends on the camera otherwise).
The RE pass reports that observe/suppress/inject variants already run natively
in the Rust combat line (x4mp-combat hitbar.rs/bulletray.rs, other worktrees);
not verified here and not part of this branch.
Out of scope v1: beams (same Bullet, 0.1 s damage-over-time loop), missiles
(Missile::Shoot 0x606f20, own detonation), mines (0x64e3c0), area damage
(0x87c380), ship-ship rams (Jolt contacts in Scene::Step). They stay native.
Prerequisite: firing for pilot slots is not applied yet (the record carries
trigger bit 0 only); outgoing claims need it first.
Feasible in this task: codec, multiplexing, staging, validation, policies,
receipt (done, tested on a fake world). Later: the four hooks, the projectile
registry for all slot-ship shots and shots near slot ships, the owner's
evaluation queries, presentation of own projectiles, the combined input
adapter.
